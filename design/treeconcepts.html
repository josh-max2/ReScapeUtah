CONCEPT A

THE RACK

Cheapest to build · most readable · scales to any number of towers
Pick a weapon off the rack, its tree fills the stage. Every tower gets the same skeleton — OUTPUT / REACH / IDENTITY, three nodes each, a capstone at every tip — so a new tower is nine lines of data, not a new screen. Good: each tree is legible on its own; progress per weapon is visible at a glance. Bad: you can never see the whole system at once, and comparing two towers means clicking twice.
CONCEPT B

THE CONSTELLATION

Most impressive · one screen · needs zoom to read
One map. COMMAND sits at the centre, and every weapon is its own small tree orbiting it — same three-branch skeleton, drawn as a cluster. Zoomed out you see the whole arsenal and how far you've got; zoom in and a cluster becomes a readable tree. Good: reads as one enormous tree, which is the fantasy; a fully-lit constellation is a trophy. Bad: needs pan/zoom to be usable, and it's the most work to build and to keep legible.
CONCEPT C

THE TECH GRID

Everything visible at once · reads as a spreadsheet, not a skill tree
Columns are weapons, rows are tiers. The whole arsenal is one table you scan left to right to compare towers, top to bottom to see depth. Tier III only unlocks once you own both nodes above it in that column. Good: total overview, trivially extensible, obvious what to buy next. Bad: it looks like a spreadsheet — the least game-like of the three, and the thing the audience is quickest to read as low-effort.
SHARED

THE TOWER-TREE SKELETON

Same in all three concepts — the concept only changes navigation
Whichever shell wins, each of the eleven towers gets the same nine-node shape. That is what keeps eleven trees from becoming eleven design problems: the branches always mean the same thing, and only the third one is bespoke to the weapon.
